## Contract from `idea.md`

1. Read the lease, amendments, projected statement, reconciliation, and ledger.
2. Extract cost rules, allocation method, gross-up, caps, capital rules, audit rights, deadlines, notice method, payment restrictions, and credit or refund rules.
3. Compute the dispute deadline first and check for a recent estoppel certificate.
4. Reconstruct estimated CAM billed and paid, actual CAM claimed, and the additional bill or credit.
5. Check permitted categories, allocation, gross-up, caps, capital treatment, duplicated fees and expenses, reimbursements, arithmetic, and supporting records.
6. Treat no flag as proven until the lease terms and available records support it.
7. Use an objection for errors visible from the statement. Use a records demand when invoices or ledgers are required.
8. Request missing evidence and state the verification limit when records do not arrive.
9. Prepare a source-backed correction request. The customer signs formal notices.
10. Send through the lease-required method, including certified mail when required.
11. Track replies, recalculate when new evidence arrives, and escalate with customer approval.
12. End with a corrected bill, credit, refund, confirmed charge, rejected dispute, or professional review.

## Human and professional boundary

- The customer controls signatures, escalation tone, compromise, and legal rights.
- A partner CPA performs and signs an audit when the lease requires an independent CPA.
- A qualified professional handles material legal or accounting interpretation.
- Terac is not required in this workflow unless a later approved task has the required qualifications and privacy controls.
