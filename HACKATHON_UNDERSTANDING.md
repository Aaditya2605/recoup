# Our Simplified Understanding of the Hackathon

## The challenge in one sentence

Build a real business where agents run the routine work needed to find customers and deliver the promised result without the founder operating each case.

## What "zero human" means to us

"Zero human" does not mean that no person can participate.

People can still be:

- Customers
- Research participants
- Experts
- Contractors
- Developers
- Marketers
- Quality-assurance testers
- Lawyers, accountants, or other qualified professionals

The important constraint is that the founder is not the routine operator. The system decides when work is required, uses software or hires a person to perform it, checks the result, and continues the company workflow.

A customer can also make decisions about the customer's own money, account, or legal rights. Removing the founder from operations does not require removing customer consent.

## The three parts of the company

Every idea must solve all three parts of a business:

### 1. Product

The company must solve a real customer problem and produce a useful result. The product can be any type of software or service.

### 2. Distribution

The company needs a repeatable way to find, contact, convert, and collect payment from customers. The founder should not manually perform every sale or marketing task.

Agents can perform outreach, publish content, qualify prospects, answer questions, manage follow-ups, or hire people to perform suitable distribution work.

### 3. Fulfillment

After a customer buys, the company must deliver the promised outcome. The founder should not manually complete each customer order or case.

Agents can use APIs, software, browsers, sandboxes, other agents, and hired specialists to complete the work. The system must manage the task through completion instead of giving the founder a plan to execute.

## Humans are a resource the company can use

An autonomous company can hire humans when humans are the correct tool for the task.

For example, an agent can:

- Recruit people to test a product
- Ask customers or general-population participants for feedback
- Hire a creator to produce marketing content
- Hire a developer to investigate a difficult bug
- Hire a qualified professional to review a legal or accounting exception
- Collect labeled data or expert judgment

The founder does not manually find, brief, remind, and manage each person. The agent initiates and manages that work as part of the company process.

## Where hackathon tools can be used

A sponsor tool does not have to appear inside the customer-facing product. It can support:

- The product itself
- Distribution
- Fulfillment
- Internal company operations
- More than one of these areas

For example, a hosting service can run the product, a QA service can test it, a model service can power agents, a sandbox can let agents operate safely, a payment service can collect revenue, and Terac can supply human input or labor.

The product idea should be selected because it solves a problem. Sponsor tools are selected afterward where the company genuinely needs them.

## What the hackathon must demonstrate

An eight-hour event cannot prove that a company can operate for months. It can demonstrate a representative end-to-end company workflow.

The strongest demonstration shows:

1. A real customer request enters the system.
2. The agent determines and performs the required work.
3. Software, other agents, or hired people complete any necessary subtasks.
4. The agent checks the work and advances the case.
5. The customer receives a real, externally verifiable result.
6. The founder does not manually operate the case behind the screen.

The external result can be a completed transaction, corrected bill, delivered service, published asset, resolved issue, or another outcome that exists outside an AI-generated report.

## What does not satisfy our interpretation

- A chatbot that gives advice while the user performs all the work
- A dashboard that displays an agent's plan but does not execute it
- A generic AI wrapper without a specific customer problem
- A product that automates creation but leaves distribution and fulfillment to the founder
- A hidden founder manually completing the workflow during the demo
- Fake payments, fake customers, or fake external actions presented as real
- Sponsor logos, API keys, or disconnected demo screens that are not part of the working company
- A task that one coding-agent prompt can already complete without an ongoing business workflow

## Official requirements versus our product thesis

The official rulebook remains the source of truth for eligibility and track requirements.

Important official requirements include:

- The project must use the Terac MCP.
- The project must use real human input collected through Terac to make the project measurably better and show a before-and-after result.
- Stripe revenue tracking is required for eligibility for the Best Overall Agent-Run Company track.
- Other sponsor tracks have their own requirements.

These requirements affect implementation and judging. They do not define which customer problem we choose to solve.

## How this applies to Recoup

### Product

Recoup identifies invalid or unsupported CAM charges and manages the correction case for a commercial tenant.

### Distribution

The company can identify suitable commercial tenants, perform outreach, explain the service, manage follow-ups, and direct interested customers to the product without the founder handling each prospect.

### Fulfillment

After the tenant submits the case, agents read the lease and reconciliation, request supporting records, verify discrepancies, send the required correction request, follow up with the landlord, and report the resolution. A qualified person can be hired for an exception that requires professional judgment.

### External result

Success is not an AI-generated lease summary. Success is one of the following:

- An invalid unpaid charge is removed.
- The tenant receives a rent credit.
- The tenant receives a refund.
- The evidence confirms that the original charge is correct.

## Our decision rule

Before building a feature, ask:

> Does this help the autonomous company acquire a customer, deliver the promised result, or safely complete a necessary company operation?

If the answer is no, the feature is not required.
